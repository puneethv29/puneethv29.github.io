How each scenario is built

Bear

The Shiller CAPE sits at ~31x today — nearly double the historical mean of ~17x. This scenario assumes investors demand a return to normal valuations over the first decade. That multiple compression (~3.5% annual headwind) overwhelms modest earnings growth, producing near-inflation returns in years 1–10.

It's not a crash — it's a slow grind where prices go sideways while earnings gradually catch up. Years 11–30 recover once the valuation drag clears, but the compounding hole from the first decade is hard to escape.

Broad yr 1–10: +1.3% div + 4.6% EPS − 3.5% P/E ≈ 2.4%
Growth tilt: higher starting multiples → deeper compression hit

Base

Multiples compress gradually to ~22x — still above the long-run mean, but arguably justified by today's S&P 500 being more profitable and capital-light than in prior eras. This "new normal" P/E is roughly where Vanguard and Research Affiliates peg their central estimates.

Earnings grow at long-run trend (~7% nominal: GDP growth + inflation + buybacks). The result is a real return around 4–5% annually after inflation — roughly what a diversified investor should expect from US equities at current valuations.

Broad yr 1–10: +1.3% div + 6.6% EPS − 2.5% P/E ≈ 5.4%
Growth tilt: lower div yield, offset by stronger EPS growth

Bull

The AI productivity thesis delivers. Large-cap tech — which dominates both broad and growth ETFs — sees earnings well above trend as AI drives margin expansion and new revenue. High multiples get justified by actual earnings, not just anticipation.

P/E barely budges because earnings acceleration keeps pace with any re-rating pressure. This resembles the 1995–2021 cycle: skeptics kept calling the top, but fundamentals kept printing. Growth tilts like QQQ see the largest upside here.

Broad yr 1–10: +1.3% div + 11.7% EPS − 3% P/E ≈ 10%
QQQ bull case: up to ~24%/yr if AI concentrates in Nasdaq
